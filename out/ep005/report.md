# Managing Remote Teams: Is “Outcomes Over Hours” Enough?

*Episode ep005 · Host: Sarah Johnson · Guests: Tom Becker, Leila Haddad*

## Summary

Host Sarah Johnson explores the central theme of whether focusing solely on outcomes rather than hours worked is sufficient for managing remote teams, featuring guests Tom Becker and Leila Haddad. Tom Becker, an engineering director managing a large, distributed team, agrees that outcomes tracking, such as quarterly goals and deliverables, is mostly effective, but cautions that outcomes alone can mask issues like burnout or invisibility. To address this, Tom suggests supplementing outcomes with weekly one-on-ones focused strictly on blockers, growth, and personal well-being, arguing that problems in remote settings often manifest as silence or reduced communication rather than noise.

Leila Haddad offered a contrasting perspective, noting that for roles like customer support, hours still matter because coverage is a key outcome, requiring scheduled shifts. However, she stressed avoiding micromanagement by avoiding activity-monitoring software, as such tools led to a collapse in morale and agent attrition. Regarding promotion bias, Tom mentioned that visibility can still be an issue, and they mitigated this by requiring written impact summaries for promotion cases, while Leila suggested rotating who presents team wins to ensure diverse voices are heard.

Ultimately, the discussion concluded that for knowledge work, outcomes over hours combined with human check-ins ('people over dashboards') is the way forward, while frontline roles might see hybrid models balancing scheduled coverage with flexibility. The consensus is that outcomes are necessary, but they must be paired with meaningful human interaction to ensure team health.

## 🔹 Top takeaways

1. Outcomes over hours are effective for knowledge work but must be paired with human check-ins.
2. Outcomes alone can mask burnout or invisibility, necessitating a deeper look at individual well-being.
3. Weekly one-on-ones should focus on blockers and growth, not just status updates.
4. Activity monitoring tools are ineffective because people learn to game them, not measure actual value.
5. Evidence, such as written impact summaries, is crucial to counter proximity bias in promotions.
