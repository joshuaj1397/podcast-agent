# Tech & Media Myths Debunked

*Episode ep005 · Host: Rachel Kim · Guests: Omar Haddad, Dr. Nina Alvarez · Recorded 2025-11-04*

## Summary

Host Rachel Kim welcomed guests Omar Haddad and Dr. Nina Alvarez to debunk common myths surrounding technology and media. The discussion began with popular media consumption, where Omar Haddad noted the massive scale of streaming services, mentioning Netflix had just over 300 million paid memberships worldwide as of the end of 2024, while Spotify boasts about 400 million monthly active users. Regarding social media, Omar countered the myth that Facebook is dying, stating it still maintains around 2 billion monthly users, though usage patterns may be changing. Dr. Nina Alvarez supported this observation, suggesting that reduced posting frequency might actually be healthier for everyone. 

The conversation shifted to cognitive myths. Dr. Alvarez strongly refuted the notion that humans only use ten percent of their brains, asserting that brain imaging confirms activity across virtually the entire brain during a normal day. The speakers also touched on attention spans, with Omar Haddad mentioning Microsoft research suggesting the average human attention span is as short as eight seconds. Dr. Alvarez agreed that while the exact figure may vary, every app is designed to capture those brief seconds of attention. Omar also provided historical context on tech, noting the iPhone launched in June 2007 and that Google acquired YouTube in 2006 for $1.65 billion, while Tim Berners-Lee invented the web in 1999. He also remarked that Elon Musk's rebranding of Twitter to X in July 2023 has not recovered the brand. 

Looking toward the future, Dr. Alvarez predicted that by 2030, more than half of everything posted online will be generated by artificial intelligence. In contrast, Omar Haddad argued that long-form audio remains the most trustworthy medium because listeners can discern when someone is bluffing.
